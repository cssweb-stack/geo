GEO Bachelor of Arts (BA)

Study People, Environments, and Geographic Patterns

The Bachelor of Arts (BA) in Geography and Environment at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa examines human societies, physical environments, and the relationships between them. Build a foundation in human and environmental geography, digital mapping, and statistical methods before developing a specialization. Courses range from tourism and food systems to climate change, water resources, GIS, and remote sensing, with regional offerings on Hawaiʻi, Asia, and the Pacific. A capstone seminar brings your geographic knowledge and research skills together.

Major at a Glance

  • Degree: Bachelor of Arts in Geography and Environment
  • Major credits: At least 31 credits, in addition to other university and college degree requirements
  • Core courses: 19 credits, including a capstone seminar
  • Specialization: At least 12 credits of GEO courses at the 300 and 400 levels, including at least 6 credits at the 400 level
  • Areas of focus: Human geography, environmental geography, and geospatial information science, with Asia Pacific regional and directed study options

Additional university and program requirements, including GPA requirements, may apply. Confirm current requirements with the Undergraduate Chair.

Core Course Requirements (19 credits)

Build a foundation in physical and human geography, digital mapping, and statistical methods, then bring those perspectives together in a capstone seminar.

  • GEO 101: The Natural Environment (3 credits)
  • GEO 101L: The Natural Environment Lab (1 credit)
  • GEO 102: World Regional Geography (3 credits) or GEO 151: Geography and Contemporary Society (3 credits)
  • GEO 104: Digital Earth (3 credits)
  • GEO 325: Geography, Environment, and Society (3 credits)
  • GEO 380: Statistical Methods in Geography (3 credits)
  • GEO 493: Capstone Undergraduate Seminar (3 credits)

Upper Division Specialization Courses (12 credits minimum)

Choose courses in human geography, environmental geography, or geospatial technology, or develop a specialization around your own interests. At least 6 credits must be at the 400 level. Courses listed below are 3 credits each unless marked as variable credits. Work with the Undergraduate Chair to plan a combination that supports your interests and meets the requirements.

Directed Studies

Explore a topic through reading, research, selected topics, or an applied field placement. Directed studies require approval and faculty supervision.

  • GEO 399: Directed Reading (variable credits)
  • GEO 468: Topics in Geography
  • GEO 490: Senior Thesis
  • GEO 492: Practicum in Geography (variable credits)

Plan Your Major

Exploring the Major?

Review the student learning outcomes to see what you will learn. Contact the Undergraduate Chair to discuss your interests, review any GEO courses you have already completed, and see how the major fits your academic goals.

Ready to Declare?

Use the university’s online declaration form to request the GEO major. Follow the instructions and complete any required approvals. Consult the Registrar’s declaration guidance for the process and effective dates. If you are adding GEO as an additional major, contact the appropriate college advising office for instructions.

Declare a GEO Major (UH login required)

Planning Your Specialization?

Work with the Undergraduate Chair to choose GEO courses that support your interests and satisfy the specialization requirements, including at least 6 credits at the 400 level. Check prerequisites and class availability before registering, and plan when to take the capstone seminar.

Getting Ready to Graduate?

Review your completed and remaining major requirements with the Undergraduate Chair. Meet with the General Education Advisor to review university and college degree requirements and confirm your graduation application steps and deadlines.
