GEO Bachelor of Arts (BA)

The Bachelor of Arts (BA) in Geography and Environment at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa examines human societies, physical environments, and the relationships between them. Build a foundation in human and environmental geography, digital mapping, and statistical methods before developing a specialization. Courses range from tourism and food systems to climate change, water resources, GIS, and remote sensing, with regional offerings on Hawaiʻi, Asia, and the Pacific. A capstone seminar brings your geographic knowledge and research skills together.

Requirements

The geography major requires at least 31 credits, including the core courses and upper-division specialization courses below.

Core Courses (19 credits)

Build a foundation in physical and human geography, digital mapping, and statistical methods, then bring those perspectives together in a capstone seminar.

  • GEO 101: The Natural Environment (3 credits)
  • GEO 101L: The Natural Environment Lab (1 credit)
  • GEO 102: World Regional Geography (3 credits) or GEO 151: Geography and Contemporary Society (3 credits)
  • GEO 104: Digital Earth (3 credits)
  • GEO 325: Geography, Environment, and Society (3 credits)
  • GEO 380: Statistical Methods in Geography (3 credits)
  • GEO 493: Capstone Undergraduate Seminar (3 credits)

Upper Division Specialization Courses (12 credits minimum)

Select courses from the list below, including at least 6 credits at the 400 level. Students are encouraged to focus on geospatial technology, human geography, or environmental geography, or to select courses that develop an appropriate level of specialization in their area of interest.

Human Geography

Investigate how culture, politics, economies, and social relationships shape places. Explore questions of development, tourism, food systems, cities, and the use of resources.

  • GEO 320: Economic Geography
  • GEO 322: Globalization and Environment
  • GEO 324: Geography of Global Tourism
  • GEO 330: Culture and Environment
  • GEO 335: Politics, Nations, and States
  • GEO 421: Urban Geography
  • GEO 422: Agriculture, Food and Society
  • GEO 423: Marine Policy
  • GEO 424: Regional Analysis
  • GEO 425: Geographies of Popular Culture
  • GEO 426: Environment, Resources and Society
  • GEO 432: Tea and Culture
  • GEO 435: Political Geography of Oceans
  • GEO 436: Geography of Peace and War
  • GEO 453: Environment and Society in China

Environmental Geography

Study the Earth’s physical systems and the ways people change them. Examine climate, water, landforms, and ecosystems alongside approaches to conservation, environmental assessment, and resource management.

  • GEO 300: Introduction to Climatology
  • GEO 302: Global Environmental Issues
  • GEO 303: General Geomorphology
  • GEO 305: Water and Society
  • GEO 309: Introduction to Biogeography
  • GEO 400: Vegetation and the Climate System
  • GEO 401: Climate Change
  • GEO 402: Agricultural Climatology
  • GEO 403: Fluvial Geomorphology
  • GEO 404: Climate, Air Quality, & Policy
  • GEO 405: Water in the Environment
  • GEO 408: Conservation and Evolutionary Biogeography
  • GEO 409: Cultural Biogeography
  • GEO 410: Human Role in Environmental Change
  • GEO 411: Past Global Change and the Human Era
  • GEO 412: Environmental Impact Assessment
  • GEO 413: Resource Management in Southeast Asia
  • GEO 415: Nature-Based Tourism Management

Geospatial Information Science

Build practical skills in collecting, analyzing, and communicating geographic information through GIS, cartography, aerial imagery, remote sensing, and spatial analysis.

  • GEO 370: UAV and Aerial Photography
  • GEO 376: Map Design and Production
  • GEO 388: Introduction to GIS
  • GEO 470: Remote Sensing
  • GEO 472: Field Mapping
  • GEO 476: Web Mapping
  • GEO 487: GIS and Spatial Analysis for Social Sciences
  • GEO 489: GIS for Environmental Sciences

Asia Pacific Region

Explore places across Asia and the Pacific, including Hawaiʻi and Honolulu, alongside a course on North America. Examine how environments, histories, cultures, and economies shape regional experiences.

  • GEO 340: Geography of North America
  • GEO 352: Geography of Japan
  • GEO 353: Geography of China
  • GEO 355: Geography of South Asia
  • GEO 356: Geography of Southeast Asia
  • GEO 365: Geography of the Pacific
  • GEO 366: Geography of Honolulu
  • GEO 368: Geography of Hawaiʻi

Directed Studies

Explore a topic through reading, research, selected topics, or an applied field placement. Directed studies require approval and faculty supervision.

  • GEO 399: Directed Reading (variable credits)
  • GEO 468: Topics in Geography
  • GEO 490: Senior Thesis
  • GEO 492: Practicum in Geography (variable credits)

Read the department’s Student Learning Outcomes to learn about the knowledge and skills students are expected to develop.
