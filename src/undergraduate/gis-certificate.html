GIS Certificate

Give Your Major a Spatial Advantage

The Undergraduate Certificate in Geospatial Information Science at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa adds practical mapping and spatial analysis skills to your major. Start with geographic information systems (GIS) and aerial image interpretation, then choose electives in cartography, remote sensing, or web and field mapping. A supervised practicum brings these skills together in an applied project. The program complements fields such as natural resource management, earth and ocean sciences, and the social and biophysical sciences.

Certificate at a Glance

  • Credential: Undergraduate Certificate in Geospatial Information Science
  • Total credits: 16 credits at the 300 and 400 levels
  • Required coursework: GEO 370 and GEO 388 (6 credits)
  • Elective coursework: Three approved courses (9 credits)
  • Applied experience: GEO 492 practicum (1 credit), with a faculty supervisor and a written report
  • Minimum grade: C or better in every certificate course
  • For GEO majors: Up to 6 credits may count toward both the major and certificate; the remaining 10 certificate credits must be unique to the certificate

Additional university and program requirements, including GPA requirements, may apply. Confirm current requirements with the Undergraduate Chair.

Required Courses (7 credits)

Build a foundation in GIS and aerial image interpretation, then bring your skills together in an applied practicum.

GEO 370: UAV and Aerial Photography (3)

Learn how unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) systems and aerial photography capture geographic information. Measure, interpret, and analyze imagery acquired by drones and other aerial platforms.

GEO 388: Introduction to GIS (3)

Build a foundation in geographic information systems, from constructing and documenting spatial databases to manipulating, analyzing, and displaying geographic data. Apply GIS techniques through student research projects.

GEO 492: Practicum in Geography (1)

Apply your geospatial skills through a field placement integrated with academic study, working on a project supported by a private company, faculty member, or research lab. You must secure a faculty supervisor and write a detailed report documenting this culminating experience.

Elective Courses (9 credits)

Choose three courses to broaden your skills or focus on an area such as applied GIS, remote sensing, spatial analysis, or web and field mapping. Students may petition the Undergraduate Chair to approve a single equivalent course outside geography worth 3 credits toward the elective course requirements.

GEO 375: Introduction to Cartography and Air Photo (3)

Explore how maps communicate geographic information through symbols, design, and map projections. Learn to compile and measure information from aerial photographs and choose effective ways to present spatial data.

GEO 470: Remote Sensing (3)

Interpret aerial photographs and satellite imagery using the principles of remote sensing. Develop image processing skills, including geometric and radiometric correction, through laboratory work and a research project.

GEO 471: 3D Mapping and Analysis (3)

Acquire, process, and analyze three dimensional geographic data from high resolution remote sensing systems. Use these techniques for environmental mapping and analysis.

GEO 472: Field Mapping (3)

Measure and record cultural and physical features in the field. Techniques include field sketching, Brunton surveying, plane table mapping, oblique photo compilation, topographic mapping, and the representation of field data.

GEO 476: Web Mapping (3)

Create interactive web maps that share and visualize geospatial data. Build on basic GIS knowledge to bring geographic information to online audiences.

GEO 487: GIS and Spatial Analysis for Social Sciences (3)

Use open source GIS tools to collect, visualize, and analyze social data. Explore spatial analysis through public health, socioeconomic, and social media data, building on basic GIS knowledge.

GEO 489: GIS for Environmental Sciences (3)

Apply GIS technologies to real world problems in the natural and environmental sciences. Develop practical skills through a research project and laboratory work.

Plan Your GIS Certificate

Starting the Certificate?

Complete the certificate application linked below, then schedule an appointment with the Undergraduate Chair. The chair will approve courses you have already completed and advise you on future courses. Check prerequisites and course availability as you plan your schedule.

Planning Your Practicum?

Secure a faculty supervisor for GEO 492 and discuss a suitable placement or project. Confirm the practicum arrangements and report expectations with your supervisor before you begin.

Ready to Complete the Certificate?

During the semester you are finishing your certificate coursework, meet with the Undergraduate Chair for approval of your completed program and to obtain the required signatures on the application. Submit the signed form with your graduation paperwork to your major college advising office.
