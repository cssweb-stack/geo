GIS Certificate

The Undergraduate Certificate in Geospatial Information Science at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa provides practical skills highly sought after by private-sector, government, and academic employers. The program is particularly relevant to students majoring in natural resource management, earth and ocean sciences, and the biophysical and social sciences. A systematic sequence of courses covers the acquisition, processing, generation, visualization, analysis, management, and dissemination of geospatial data. The certificate requires 16 credits, including five upper-division courses at the 300–400 level and a one-credit practicum.

Required Courses (7 credits)

Required courses establish a foundation in geographic information systems and image interpretation, the core of geospatial information science. A practicum provides applied experience through an integrative capstone project.

All certificate courses must be completed with a grade of C or better. Geography and Environment majors may count up to 6 credits toward both the major and certificate; the remaining 10 certificate credits must be unique to the certificate.

GEO 370: UAV and Aerial Photography (3)

Learn about UAV (unmanned aerial vehicle) systems and the measurement, interpretation, analysis, and use of photography acquired by UAVs and other aerial systems.

GEO 388: Introduction to GIS (3)

Build an introductory foundation in GIS for importing, managing, analyzing, and disseminating spatial data using computer database and information technology.

GEO 492: Practicum in Geography (1)

Develop geospatial information science skills through a field placement integrated with academic study and an applied project supported by a private company, faculty member, or research lab. Students must secure a faculty member willing to supervise the practicum and write a detailed report documenting this culminating experience.

Elective Courses (9 credits)

Optional courses develop a broad range of skills or significant depth in a specialty such as applied geographic information systems, advanced remote sensing techniques, data analytics, or web and field mapping. Students may petition the Undergraduate Chair to approve one equivalent three-credit course outside geography toward the certificate’s optional course requirements.

GEO 375: Introduction to Cartography and Air Photo (3)

Study cartography, the art and science of creating maps to represent and communicate spatial information. Explore layout, colors, symbols, and typography, and practice designing maps for clarity and visual appeal.

GEO 470: Remote Sensing (3)

Study the processing and analysis of high-, medium-, and coarse-spatial-resolution geospatial data from satellite platforms for environmental mapping at scales ranging from landscapes to the globe.

GEO 471: 3D Mapping and Analysis (3)

Study the acquisition, processing, and analysis of three-dimensional remotely sensed data for resource and environmental mapping, management, and decision making.

GEO 472: Field Mapping (3)

Learn techniques for measuring and recording cultural and physical data in the field, including field sketching, Brunton surveying, plane table mapping, oblique photo compilation, topographic mapping, and representation of field data.

GEO 476: Web Mapping (3)

Learn to develop web-based GIS applications for sharing and visualizing geospatial data on the Internet. Topics include web-GIS service development, geodatabase management, and web-based geovisualization.

GEO 487: GIS and Spatial Analysis for Social Sciences (3)

Develop analytical skills for exploring, visualizing, and mining geospatial data, with an emphasis on cutting-edge techniques for handling large quantities of geospatial data.

GEO 489: GIS for Environmental Sciences (3)

Investigate GIS applications to problems and issues in the social, natural, and environmental sciences through research projects and a laboratory.
