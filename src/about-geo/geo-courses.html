Discover geography through a course. Whether you are exploring an elective, building skills to complement another major, or pursuing a GEO degree, our courses connect people, places, and the environment. Explore Hawaiʻi, Asia, and the Pacific, investigate social and environmental questions, and learn to see the world through maps, fieldwork, and geographic research.

Introductory Courses

Our 100-level courses introduce the natural environment, world regions, the connections between geography and contemporary society, and the ways maps represent our planet. Explore weather, climate, landscapes, and cultures while building a foundation for further study. Courses such as The Natural Environment, World Regional Geography, and Digital Earth offer starting points for students discovering geography or connecting it with another field.

Upper Division Courses

Our 300- and 400-level courses deepen your understanding of climate, water, cities, cultures, tourism, and environmental change. Explore the geography of Hawaiʻi and other world regions, or develop practical skills in GIS, cartography, remote sensing, and spatial analysis. Courses such as Water and Society, Geography of Global Tourism, and Web Mapping connect geographic ideas with current questions, while fieldwork, research, and practicum courses provide opportunities to put learning into practice. Check individual course prerequisites as you plan your studies.

Graduate Courses

Our 600- and 700-level courses bring geographic theory, research design, and specialized methods into advanced study. Examine topics such as political ecology, coastal management, migration, and community-based natural resource management, with opportunities to focus on Hawaiʻi, Asia, and the Pacific. Seminars encourage discussion and critical analysis, while directed research and thesis research support independent investigation in collaboration with faculty.
