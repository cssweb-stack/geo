Explore how people and environments shape one another—and develop the research skills to investigate the questions that matter to you. The MA in Geography and Environment at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa brings together human geography, environmental geography, and geospatial technologies. Work with a faculty advisor to build a specialization around your interests and carry out an original research project, in a setting with strong connections to Hawaiʻi, the Pacific, and Asia.

What will you explore?

How does tourism reshape communities? Who has access to land and water? How can satellite imagery help us understand environmental change? Your interests and the expertise of your faculty advisor help shape the direction of your studies.

Human geography

Investigate the relationships among people, power, and place. Explore topics such as migration, borders, tourism, development, culture, and the social dimensions of environmental change.

Environmental geography

Study environmental processes and the decisions that affect them. Interests may include biogeography, water resources, conservation, coastal management, and the relationships between communities and natural resources.

Geospatial technologies

Use GIS, remote sensing, and spatial analysis to investigate geographic questions. Develop approaches to mapping and analyzing patterns in social and environmental data.

Shape your own research

You will work with a faculty advisor to choose a coherent set of courses and develop a research project supported by departmental expertise. Core seminars introduce geographic ideas, help you get to know faculty research, and guide you in turning an interest into a research proposal.

Build skills suited to your question, whether that means qualitative research, quantitative analysis, field methods, or laboratory techniques. Your thesis gives you the opportunity to bring those skills together: gather and analyze evidence, develop an argument, and communicate your findings.

Study in Hawaiʻi, with connections across the Asia-Pacific

Hawaiʻi’s environmental diversity and historical and cultural context offer a compelling setting for geographic inquiry. Departmental interests extend across Hawaiʻi, the Pacific, and Asia, connecting questions about particular places with broader debates about environmental change, resource management, and society.

Build toward your next step

The program develops skills in research design, analysis, and writing that can support work in environmental planning, natural resource management, public policy, consulting, and nonprofit organizations, as well as further graduate study.

Students from related disciplines are welcome; an undergraduate degree in geography is not required. Advisors help incoming students identify any additional preparation needed in geography, geospatial technologies, or quantitative methods.
