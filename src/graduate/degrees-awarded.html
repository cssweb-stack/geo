The Department of Geography and Environment has a long tradition of graduate scholarship spanning generations of research in Hawaiʻi, the Pacific, and beyond. The master's theses and doctoral dissertations represented in the department's historical records reflect the diversity of geographic inquiry and the changing questions, methods, and challenges that have shaped the discipline. Explore these collections to discover the breadth of research conducted through our graduate programs.

MA Master of Arts

Master's Degree Recipients

Since 1930, master's research in the department has explored the complex relationships among people, places, and environments. Topics represented in the department's records range from the geography of Hawaiʻi and the Pacific to cultural landscapes, migration, urban development, environmental conservation, and geospatial technologies. This body of scholarship reflects a longstanding tradition of geographic inquiry and an evolving understanding of our world.

PhD Doctor of Philosophy

Doctoral Degree Recipients

Since 1971, doctoral research in the department has addressed questions spanning human geography, physical geography, environmental change, and geospatial science. Dissertation topics represented in the department's records include climate, ecosystems, cultural and political geography, economic development, migration, and land-use change. Together, these works illustrate the breadth of geographic scholarship conducted in Hawaiʻi, the Pacific, and beyond.
