Geography and Environment PhD: Exams & Proposal

Review comprehensive examinations, dissertation proposal approval, and the associated examination and presentation protocols.

Exams and Proposal

Comprehensive Exams

Written and Oral Comprehensive Exams are required by the graduate program. Comprehensive exams assess whether the doctoral student has attained an adequate level of geographical knowledge to continue with dissertation research, and are taken after the student has completed their coursework requirements. The exams consist of questions directly related to the student’s specialization, but may also test a general understanding of the history of geographic thought, the nature of critical inquiry, and research methodologies appropriate to the student’s field.

Each doctoral student will begin the comprehensive exams with the development of a bibliography in consultation with each Advisory Committee member. This bibliography constitutes the core knowledge upon which the written and oral examination will be based.

  • Written Exam — The Advisory Committee will determine the format and content of the written exam. Each Advisory Committee member may ask a question or a series of questions. The written exam will be carried out on five consecutive workdays. Each day, the student will answer question(s) from one committee member within four hours. The written exam can be open book or closed book, at each committee member’s discretion.
  • Oral Exam — The oral exam will take up to three hours. The Advisory Committee will determine the format and content of the exam. The student will be asked to clarify and elaborate on written exam answers, and may also be asked other questions on their specialization, or general questions on philosophy or methodologies in the discipline of geography.

A student passes the comprehensive exams when a majority of the Advisory Committee that includes the Committee Chair votes in favor of a positive assessment. A student who fails the comprehensive exams can repeat once. A student who fails to pass the comprehensive exams for the second time will be dropped from the program.

Approval of Dissertation Proposal

Upon passing the comprehensive exams, the doctoral student prepares a formal dissertation proposal for review by the Advisory Committee. Students are required to orally present their proposal before the Department.

The Advisory Committee must approve the proposal, and this can require multiple revisions. Once approved there should be regular consultation between the Advisory Committee and student concerning progress in the dissertation research. Students should maintain consistent communication and inform the Advisory Committee of any significant deviations from their approved proposal.

The student must pass the comprehensive exams and have the proposal approved by the Advisory Committee in order to file Form II with the Graduate Division and advance to candidacy. Changes to the Advisory Committee after the filing of Form II must be approved by the Graduate Chair in consultation with all the current and proposed committee members on the Petition to Revise the Doctoral Committee.

  • The committee chair (or her/his designate) should email one question each day to the candidate at an agreed-upon time.
  • The candidate should email back the answer to the committee chair within 4 hours after receiving each question.

The oral exam will take up to three hours. The Advisory Committee will determine the format and content of the exam. A student who fails to pass the comprehensive exams for the second time will be dropped from the program.

Committee participation in the oral exams can also be done using remote technology (Zoom/Google hangout/Skype). The student should coordinate with his/her committee chair to arrange in advance the platform on which the committee will meet.

Protocol to Conduct PhD Oral Exam

  • The exam is a closed meeting between the student and members of her/his advisory committee
  • After everybody arrives, the committee chair starts the meeting by reiterating the intent of the comprehensive exams, and the procedure of the written exams that the student has completed.
  • The chair asks the student to leave the room. If meeting virtually, the committee chair should arrange for a separate and secure breakout room.
  • Each committee member gives their respective assessment of the written part of the exams. The committee then discusses and decides whether the oral examination should proceed. If yes, the committee decides the order of questioning for the first round.
  • The student is called back into the room and questioning will proceed
  • The student is first given a chance to reflect on her/his written answer and elaborate/retract (if any)
  • When one round has been completed, open opportunities for the second round. Any member can ask at any time, especially if the question is related to the one being discussed.
  • At the end of the questioning, the student will leave the room again while the committee discusses the exams.
  • Forms have been merged into one Form II using online electronic forms with Kuali Build (https://build.kuali.co/). Committee members signed their approval after a student passes the comprehensive exams and receives approval for the dissertation proposal. A successful filing for Form II is required before a student can register for GEO 800.
  • The committee chair should inform the Graduate Chair (via email or any other forms of communication) on the outcome of the comprehensive exams. Students who never had a Master’s degree in Geography have the option of requesting an MA degree in Geography be awarded on the basis of passing the comprehensive exams en-route to PhD.

Dissertation Research Proposal

Upon passing the comprehensive exams, the doctoral student prepares a formal dissertation proposal for review by the Advisory Committee. PhD students are required to orally present their proposal before the Department.

Protocol for Proposal Presentation

Proposal presentation is open to the departmental community and should be announced through the appropriate listservs at least one week in advance.

  • Under COVID-19 special rule, remote participation of the graduate student and all members of the committee is allowed.
  • The student, in coordination with the committee chair, is responsible to make prior arrangements for the virtual presentation.
  • All materials, including any visual aids, have been distributed in advance to all committee members.
  • The committee chair starts the meeting by welcoming everyone, introducing the student, and explaining how the proposal presentation will proceed.
  • The candidate presents the proposal in 30 minutes or less.
  • The presentation will be followed by a short Q&A to clarify the presentation (questions could be posed by the general audience or the advisory committee)
  • The committee chair thanks the audience and request them to leave the room
  • The committee will then have a close meeting with the student and give feedback to the proposal
  • After the proposal is approved, the committee will sign Form 2 and the student is eligible to sign up for GEO 800.
