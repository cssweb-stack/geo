GEO Doctor of Philosophy (PhD)

Advance Your Geographic Research

The Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) in Geography and Environment at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa centers on independent research and an original contribution to geographic knowledge. Develop an individually arranged specialization with a faculty advisor and advisory committee, deepening your command of its literature, theories, and methods. Tailor your research training through approaches such as GIS, remote sensing, qualitative inquiry, or fieldwork. Coursework, comprehensive exams, and proposal development prepare you to undertake a dissertation and communicate your findings through writing and a public defense.

Doctorate at a Glance

  • Degree: Doctor of Philosophy (PhD)
  • Program length: Designed for approximately four to five years; timing varies with prior preparation and the research project
  • Focus: An individually arranged specialization supported by faculty expertise
  • Key milestones: Coursework, written and oral comprehensive exams, and an approved dissertation proposal
  • Research outcome: An original dissertation and public defense

This page summarizes program highlights and does not list every requirement. Applicants and students are responsible for reviewing and meeting all applicable admission, degree, and graduation requirements and deadlines. Review the PhD Handbook for coursework and research requirements, course restrictions, approvals, and additional requirements; review UH Mānoa Graduate Division policies and degree requirements; and confirm your plans with the Graduate Chair and your faculty advisor. Requirements and procedures may change; confirm current information before applying, registering, or planning graduation.

Read the PhD Handbook

Courses and Research

Your doctoral studies combine a foundation in geographic ideas, a specialization shaped around your interests, and methods training that supports your research. Work with your advisor and advisory committee to connect your coursework with an original dissertation.

Required Core (4 credits)

Meet the department’s faculty and explore the concepts and theories that shape geographic research. The core introduces you to faculty research and helps place your own interests within the discipline.

  • GEO 692: Faculty Seminar Series (1 credit)
  • GEO 695: Concepts and Theories of Geography (3 credits)

These courses may be waived if you previously completed them during an MA program at UH Mānoa. Confirm applicability with the Graduate Chair.

Specialization (15 credits minimum)

Develop a focused course plan that connects your research interests with faculty expertise. Deepen your knowledge of the literature, theories, and findings in your chosen specialty through coursework approved by your advisory committee. Approved coursework from a previous master’s degree may count toward part of this requirement.

Research Skills (6 credits minimum)

Choose methods training suited to your dissertation research. Options may include research design, GIS, remote sensing, statistics, qualitative methods, fieldwork, or laboratory techniques. Your advisory committee approves the courses that support your project; foreign language competency may also be required when essential to your research.

Dissertation Research

Bring your specialization and research skills together in an original contribution to geographic knowledge. After completing comprehensive exams and gaining approval for your proposal, conduct independent research with guidance from your advisor and advisory committee. Analyze your findings, write your dissertation, and present your work in a public defense.

Plan Your PhD

Exploring the Program?

Before applying, review faculty research interests and identify how your proposed work connects with departmental expertise. We welcome applicants who can explain that connection and the questions they hope to investigate. Applicants from related fields are welcome; additional coursework may be needed to address gaps in geographic knowledge or methods. Contact the Graduate Chair to discuss potential advisors, program fit, and preparation for doctoral study.

Ready to Apply?

Review the UH Graduate Division admissions standards and documentation requirements, then prepare your application materials:

  • A 1- to 2-page statement of objectives outlining your research goals, relevant experience, and plans.
  • Three recommendation letters, with academic references preferred.
  • GRE scores (recommended but not required).
  • Academic transcripts as needed from each postsecondary institution attended.
  • English proficiency scores, if required.
  • A writing sample is required for PhD applicants.

Submit your application (fee required) for fall admission only. The application deadline is January 15.

Apply through UH Mānoa Graduate Division

Direct application process questions to UH Graduate Division and program questions to the Graduate Chair.

Planning Your Coursework and Research?

Work with your faculty advisor and advisory committee to plan your specialization and research methods training. Use the PhD Handbook to prepare for comprehensive exams, proposal approval, and dissertation research. Browse doctoral dissertation topics, explore graduate funding sources, and check the course resources below when planning your schedule.

Getting Ready to Graduate?

Review your remaining requirements and dissertation milestones with your advisor and advisory committee. Check Graduate Division’s graduation instructions and deadlines as you plan your public defense, dissertation submission, and application for graduation. Confirm required approvals, forms, and final-semester registration with the Graduate Chair.
