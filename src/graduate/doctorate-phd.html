Develop an original contribution to how we understand people, places, and environmental change. The PhD in Geography and Environment at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa combines an individually arranged program of study with independent research guided by a faculty advisor and advisory committee. Deepen your expertise in a geographic specialty while building the methods, critical thinking, and communication skills to contribute to research, public service, and higher education.

Degree at a Glance

  • Degree: Doctor of Philosophy (PhD)
  • Program length: Designed for approximately four to five years; timing varies with prior preparation and the research project
  • Focus: An individually arranged specialization supported by faculty expertise
  • Research outcome: An original dissertation and public defense

Research Questions That Matter to You

Investigate questions such as how borders shape mobility, how communities govern land and water, or how environmental change can be understood through ecological records and spatial data. Faculty interests span political and cultural geography, migration, tourism, political ecology, biogeography, water resources, conservation, GIS, and remote sensing, with strong connections to Hawaiʻi, Asia, and the Pacific.

Work with your advisor to shape a coherent specialization and choose methods suited to your research. These may include qualitative inquiry, quantitative analysis, GIS and remote sensing, field methods, or laboratory techniques. Through your dissertation, you will formulate a research problem, analyze evidence, and communicate findings in writing and through oral presentation.

Explore faculty research interests and browse the department’s doctoral dissertation list to see the range of questions students have pursued.

Connect with the Program

Prospective students: Contact the department at uhmgeog@hawaii.edu to discuss your research interests, potential faculty advisors, and how to apply. Applicants from related fields are welcome; an undergraduate geography major is not required.

Current students: Use the handbook with your advisor to plan coursework and research milestones. Explore graduate funding sources and check the course resources below when planning each semester.
