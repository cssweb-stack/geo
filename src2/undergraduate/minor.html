GEO Minor

Add a Geographic Perspective to Your Major

A GEO minor helps you connect the subjects you study with the places, communities, and environments they affect. Explore issues such as climate change, tourism, resource use, and global inequality while developing skills in mapping and spatial analysis. With courses spanning human geography, environmental geography, and geospatial technologies, you can build a minor that complements your interests and helps you understand challenges in Hawaiʻi and beyond.

Minor at a Glance

  • Program: Minor in Geography and Environment
  • Total credits: 15 credits of GEO coursework at the 300 and 400 levels
  • Required breadth: At least one course in each of three areas: human geography, environmental geography, and geospatial technologies
  • Additional coursework: Select further courses to reach 15 credits after meeting the three area requirements
  • Planning: Choose courses with the Undergraduate Chair that complement your major and interests

ⓘAdditional university and program requirements, including GPA requirements, may apply. Confirm current requirements with the Undergraduate Chair.

Area Requirements (9 credits minimum)

Choose at least one GEO course from each of the three areas below, then select additional GEO courses to reach 15 credits. All courses listed below are 3 credits each. Each area offers a different perspective on the relationships between people and their environment.

  • GEO 421: Urban Geography
  • GEO 422: Agriculture, Food and Society
  • GEO 423: Marine Policy
  • GEO 424: Regional Analysis
  • GEO 425: Geographies of Popular Culture
  • GEO 426: Environment, Resources and Society
  • GEO 432: Tea and Culture
  • GEO 435: Political Geography of Oceans
  • GEO 436: Geography of Peace and War
  • GEO 453: Environment and Society in China

Environmental Geography

Examine the processes that shape the natural environment and the choices people make about it, from climate and water to conservation and resource management.

  • GEO 305: Water and Society
  • GEO 309: Introduction to Biogeography
  • GEO 400: Vegetation and the Climate System
  • GEO 401: Climate Change
  • GEO 402: Agricultural Climatology
  • GEO 403: Fluvial Geomorphology
  • GEO 404: Climate, Air Quality, & Policy
  • GEO 405: Water in the Environment
  • GEO 408: Conservation and Evolutionary Biogeography
  • GEO 409: Cultural Biogeography
  • GEO 410: Human Role in Environmental Change
  • GEO 411: Past Global Change and the Human Era
  • GEO 412: Environmental Impact Assessment

Geospatial Technologies

Develop ways to investigate and communicate geographic patterns through GIS, maps, aerial imagery, remote sensing, and spatial analysis.

  • GEO 370: UAV and Aerial Photography
  • GEO 376: Map Design and Production

  • GEO 470: Remote Sensing
  • GEO 472: Field Mapping
  • GEO 476: Web Mapping
  • GEO 487: GIS and Spatial Analysis for Social Sciences
  • GEO 489: GIS for Environmental Sciences

Plan Your Minor

Exploring the Minor?

Contact the Undergraduate Chair to discuss your interests and review any GEO courses you have already taken. Plan a combination that meets the three area requirements and fits alongside your major. Check prerequisites and class availability before registering.

Ready to Declare?

Submit the university’s online declaration form to add the GEO minor to your academic record. Follow the form’s instructions and complete any required approvals. The Registrar’s declaration guidance explains the process and when your request takes effect.

Declare a GEO Minor (UH login required)

Getting Ready to Graduate?

Review your completed and remaining courses with the Undergraduate Chair to confirm that you have met the minor requirements. Check with the General Education Advisor that the minor is included in your graduation review and ask whether any additional completion paperwork is needed.
